# Gout Crystal All-Atom Molecular Dynamics

Simulating monosodium urate (MSU), the crystal behind gout, one atom at a time.

**Why it matters:** right now, about 56 million people are living with gout, and that number is on track to hit 96 million by 2050 (GBD 2021, *Lancet Rheumatology* 2024). It's one of the most painful conditions known to medicine. Attacks have been compared to a broken bone, often striking the big toe in the middle of the night. Yet most people have no idea what it actually is. Blame gets pinned on beer, but sugary soft drinks are just as guilty. The real culprit is invisible: uric acid quietly builds up in the blood until it crystallizes into microscopic needles of monosodium urate packed into the joints. Left alone, those needles pile up over years into chalky lumps called tophi that can wreck bone and cartilage.

**What it does:** all-atom molecular dynamics of MSU crystals falling out of solution and growing on collagen, the protein they seed on in joints. It also looks at how they dissolve at different urate levels and temperatures.

**Where it's going:** once the crystal model is solid, the plan is to simulate how different █████████ and █████████ dissolve the crystals, and how █████████, █████████ and other █████████ can █████████ them.

**Why █████████ them:** a crystal dissolves faster the more surface it exposes (the Noyes–Whitney equation), and very small fragments are more soluble than large crystals (the Gibbs–Thomson, or Ostwald–Freundlich, effect). Breaking tophi into fine fragments should let them dissolve far faster, especially when combined with █████████. The fresh fragments may cause a temporary immune response, but the aim is to clear tophi faster than current methods allow.

This is the public side of a private repository. The code, data and results are kept there and aren't published.
